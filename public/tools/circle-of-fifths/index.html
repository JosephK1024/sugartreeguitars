The circle of fifths, for people who play by ear

It looks like music-theory wallpaper, but it's really a compass: every key's chord family sits right next to it, and the keys that blend smoothly are always neighbors on the wheel. Click around below — it updates for guitar as you go.

Starting point: you don't need to read the wheel top to bottom. Pick the key you already play in most (probably G, C, D, A, E, or one of their minors), click it, and read the panel on the right.

Why the wheel is shaped like this

Start on C and go up a fifth — five notes up the alphabet, wrapping as needed — and you land on G. Go up a fifth from G and you land on D. Keep stacking fifths and you eventually touch all twelve keys and land back on C, which is why it's a circle instead of a line. Each step clockwise adds exactly one sharp to the key signature; each step counterclockwise adds one flat. That's the whole mechanism — the wheel isn't decoration, it's just every key sorted by how many accidentals it has.

The reason this matters for songwriting: the two chords sitting next to any key on the wheel are that key's IV and V chords — the two chords, besides the root itself, that almost every chord progression leans on. Pick a key, look one wedge either side of it, and you're looking at three-quarters of the "Wonderwall" chord vocabulary before you've thought about theory at all.

Reading a chord family

The seven chords in the panel run I – ii – iii – IV – V – vi – vii°. Capital-numeral chords (I, IV, V) are major; lowercase (ii, iii, vi) are minor; the little circle (vii°) is a diminished chord you'll mostly use in passing, not as a resting point. Almost every folk, pop, rock, and country song is built from I, IV, V, and vi — that's four chords out of the seven, and they're always the biggest, most useful ones in the family.

Clicking the inner ring instead reframes things around the relative minor — same seven notes, same seven chords, but counted from the vi chord instead, so it becomes i. Minor-key songs usually lean on i, iv, v, VI, and VII. One common trick: swap the minor v for a major V (in A minor, that's E instead of Em) right before returning to i — it pulls harder toward home. That's a borrowed note from the harmonic minor scale, and it's why so many minor-key songs use a chord that technically isn't "in the key."

Chord spelling follows standard key-signature notation (so F♯ major is spelled with a theoretical E♯ rather than F — guitarists will only ever see this in the rarest keys). Capo suggestions favor the lowest fret that lets you play fully open C, D, E, G, A, Am, Dm, or Em shapes.

What’s in it

Nine tunings

Standard, Double Drop D, Modal E, Modal D, DADGAD, Open G, Open Gsus4, Open C6, and the Crosby tuning. Every chord and scale diagram redraws for the tuning you pick.

Chord shapes you can trust

Each voicing is brute-force checked to sound only real chord tones with the root in the bass — never a hand-guessed shape. Flip any chord between its E-shape and A-shape.

All seven modes

Ionian through Locrian, built on your key's own tonic and spelled from its own letter, each with a fretboard diagram, note names, and a note on the mood it carries.

Four genre sounds

Plain/Folk, Jazz, Blues, and Punk reshape the whole chord family at once — and the progressions list follows along, so a 12-bar blues reads G7–C7–D7 instead of G–C–D.

Embellishment chips

Swap any chord for add9, maj7, sus2, sus4, m7, m9, dom7, or a power chord, with a caption on the feel it brings. Which chips appear depends on the chord's real role in the key.

Capo positions

For any key, the lowest capo fret that lets you play fully open C, D, E, G, A, Am, Dm, or Em shapes — so you can stay in the shapes your hands already know.

Positions up the neck

Walk any scale or mode through every position it naturally falls into, root-anchored, from the nut to the top of the neck. Your choices are remembered as you change keys.

A place for song ideas

Catch chord and note snippets in a scratchpad while you noodle, then export the lot when something starts to turn into a song.
